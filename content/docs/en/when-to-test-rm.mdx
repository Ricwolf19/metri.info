Testing a max is a skill and a stressor, not a workout. Done at the right
moment it tells you where you stand and sets the next block's numbers; done at
the wrong one it costs a week of recovery and returns a number fatigue had
already discounted.

## What to test

| Test     | What it gives                     | Who                                                      |
| -------- | --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| True 1RM | One maximal single                | Lifters with years under the bar, safeties and a spotter |
| 3–5RM    | A heavy triple or five, converted | Everyone else                                            |

A rep max in the 3–5 range carries most of the information of a true max at a
fraction of the risk, and its estimate lands within a few percent. The
[1RM calculator](/tools/1rm-calculator) does the arithmetic:

```
e1RM = weight × (1 + reps ÷ 30)
```

## When

- **The last week of a block**, or the first after a deload, once the fatigue
  of the previous weeks is gone. The Metri app marks that week on the routine
  as an RM test week.
- **Before a phase that prescribes percentages**: the number you test is the
  number the coming weeks are built on.
- **Never mid-block or tired**: a test on top of accumulated volume reads 5–10%
  low, then drags every percentage down with it.
- **Not after a layoff**: two weeks back first. A test on the first day back
  measures rust, not strength.
- Once per block is plenty. More often and the test becomes the training.

## How

| Set      | Load  | Reps | Rest    |
| -------- | ----- | ---- | ------- |
| 1        | ~40%  | 5    | 2 min   |
| 2        | ~60%  | 3    | 2–3 min |
| 3        | ~75%  | 2    | 3 min   |
| 4        | ~85%  | 1    | 3–5 min |
| 5        | ~92%  | 1    | 3–5 min |
| Attempts | +2–5% | 1    | 3–5 min |

Three attempts at most. The test IS the session for that lift: log it and keep
the rest of the day light.

<Callout type="warning">
  Use safeties or a spotter on anything over your head or on your back. A
  missed max without them is how a test turns into an injury.
</Callout>

## What to do with the number

Write it where the next block reads it, then forget it for four weeks: the
prescriptions and the RIR do the work. If the estimate from your heaviest
working set (the Metri app shows it on every exercise as e1RM) is already above
the test, trust the training, not the test day.
