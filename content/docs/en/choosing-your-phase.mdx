Whether you should be eating above, below or at maintenance is decided by your
body-fat percentage, not by the calendar or your mood. Body fat determines
whether a surplus turns into muscle or into more fat, and whether a deficit
costs you strength. Get the phase right and the diet almost writes itself.

<Callout type="tip">
  Estimate where you are with the
  [body-fat calculator](/tools/body-fat-calculator), then read your number in
  the [body-fat guide](/docs/body-fat-guide).
</Callout>

## Men

| Body fat | Phase                                                     |
| -------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| 6–12%    | Surplus: you have room to gain                            |
| 12–15%   | Optimal band: maintain, or cut lightly (300–400 g/wk)     |
| 15–20%   | Deficit; you can still add muscle and strength            |
| 20%+     | Deficit, no debate (400–500 g/wk), until back in the band |

The long-term rhythm is to bulk up to ~15%, cut back to ~12%, and repeat.
**Never bulk past 18–20%**: beyond that the surplus mostly goes to fat.

## Women

| Body fat | Phase                                          |
| -------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| 10–12%   | Surplus until ~20%, then switch to maintenance |
| 20–22%   | Maintain and recompose                         |
| ~25%     | Deficit, ~400 g per week                       |
| 30%+     | Deficit, up to 500 g per week                  |

**Never bulk past 28–30%.** A woman at ~20% who wants to get leaner cuts
slowly, ~300 g per week.

## Why a band, not a single number

The optimal band is where you are not hungry, recover well, sleep well and keep
performing in the gym. Above it, extra fat worsens
[insulin sensitivity](/docs/insulin-sensitivity) and makes the next kilo of fat
easier to gain. Below it, recovery, training and sleep start to suffer. That is
why the band has a floor as well as a ceiling, and why nobody should stay in a
cut indefinitely.

## How fast to gain

More food does not mean more muscle. The rate is capped by how much fat you
are willing to gain, and that cap drops as you get more experienced.

| Level        | Gain per month    | At 80 kg   |
| ------------ | ----------------- | ---------- |
| Beginner     | 1–1.5% bodyweight | 0.8–1.2 kg |
| Intermediate | 0.5–1%            | 0.4–0.8 kg |
| Advanced     | 0.25–0.5%         | 0.2–0.4 kg |

A true beginner can run faster in the first month or two, because much of that
gain is glycogen and water filling muscles that just started training.

Check the tape before reacting to the scale. After two or three weeks, if the
weight is climbing faster than planned, measure where you store fat first: the
waist for men, thighs and glutes for women. If those sites have barely moved,
keep the calories. If they grow while the lifts do not, you are above your
ceiling: gain slower or switch to maintenance.

The opposite happens too. As you gain weight and muscle, the old surplus slowly
becomes your new maintenance
([adaptive thermogenesis](/docs/adaptive-thermogenesis)). If the scale and the
lifts both stall for a few weeks while the waist holds, add a little food.

## How fast to lose

| Body fat  | Ceiling per month  | At 80 kg                    |
| --------- | ------------------ | --------------------------- |
| Above 25% | 3–4% of bodyweight | 2.4–3.2 kg (~0.6–0.8 kg/wk) |
| 13–19%    | 1–2%               | 0.8–1.6 kg (~200–400 g/wk)  |

These are upper limits, not targets. The leaner you are, the slower you must
lose: the risk of losing muscle rises as fat drops. Around **400 g per week**
works for most people (300–350 g if you are light, ~500 g if you are heavy or
have a lot to lose). Faster loss brings more hunger, worse sessions and sleep,
and a hormonal cost; it also does not guarantee more fat lost, since much of a
fast drop is glycogen and water.

## Recomposition at maintenance

Gaining muscle and gaining weight are not the same thing. Inside the optimal
band you do not need to pick a direction: eating at maintenance while training
hard slowly swaps fat for muscle at the same bodyweight. A man at 75 kg and 15%
can sit at 75 kg and 13% six months later; a woman can go from 22% to 19% the
same way. If you are not hungry, recover well, sleep well and the lifts keep
moving, there is no reason to raise calories.

<Callout type="info">
  These bands are coaching ranges, not diagnoses. Body-fat estimates carry an
  error of a few points, so use the same method every time and act on the
  trend.
</Callout>
