Macros (protein, carbohydrate and fat) are where your daily calories come
from. Get the split right and body composition follows; obsess over food choice
while ignoring totals and you'll spin your wheels.

Every gram carries a fixed amount of energy: **4 kcal** for protein, **4** for
carbs, **9** for fat. Hit your macro grams and you hit your calories by
definition.

## Start with calories

Your macro plan sits on top of a calorie target. Use the
[TDEE calculator](/tools/tdee-calculator) to estimate maintenance, then adjust:

- **Cut:** ~10% below maintenance
- **Maintain:** at maintenance
- **Lean bulk:** ~10% above maintenance

Which of the three, and how fast, depends on your body fat; see
[Cut, bulk or maintain?](/docs/choosing-your-phase).
[BMR & TDEE explained](/docs/tdee) covers where the maintenance number comes
from.

## Set protein first

Protein is the priority macro: it preserves muscle in a deficit and builds it
in a surplus. Size it one of two ways.

**Per kilogram of bodyweight**: simple, and accurate enough at a normal
body-fat level.

| Goal     | Protein               |
| -------- | --------------------- |
| Cut      | 2.2 g / kg bodyweight |
| Maintain | 2.0 g / kg            |
| Bulk     | 1.8 g / kg            |

**Per kilogram of lean mass**: better when you carry a lot of fat, because fat
tissue doesn't need protein to maintain itself. Use **1.8–2.5 g per kg of lean
mass**, with 2.2 as the default. Example: 100 kg at 20% body fat carries 20 kg
of fat, so lean mass is 80 kg and 80 × 2.2 = **176 g** a day.

```
lean_kg   = weight_kg × (1 − body_fat)
protein_g = lean_kg × 2.2
```

<Callout type="tip">
  176 g of protein is not 176 g of chicken: meat and fish hold roughly 20–25 g
  per 100 g. Enter your body fat in the
  [macro calculator](/tools/macro-calculator) and it switches to the lean-mass
  method on its own.
</Callout>

## Then fat

Fat supports hormones and vitamin absorption. Set it in grams per kg of
bodyweight, not as a percentage of calories.

| Fat (g / kg) | When                                               |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------- |
| 0.5          | The floor: never lower, not even in a cut          |
| 0.7          | Cutting, so more of the budget goes to carbs       |
| 0.8          | Maintaining or recomposing                         |
| 0.9–1        | Bulking                                            |
| 1.2–1.5      | Hard-gainers who struggle to eat enough            |
| 1.5–2        | Low-carb diets, where fat has to fill the calories |

The first four rows are what the macro calculator uses. If you carry a lot of
fat, sizing fat on lean mass instead works too; it just leaves a little more
room for carbs.

## Then carbs fill the rest

Whatever calories remain after protein and fat become carbohydrate. Keeping fat
toward the low end when cutting lands more of the budget here, and carbs are
what fuel hard sessions.

| Carbs (g / kg) | Profile                 |
| -------------- | ----------------------- |
| 2 or less      | Keto / low-carb         |
| 2–3            | Low                     |
| 3–5            | Moderate: most lifters  |
| 5–8            | High: intense endurance |
| 8–10           | Ultra-endurance events  |

## Worked example: 2,500 kcal

100 kg, 20% body fat (80 kg lean), cutting on 2,500 kcal:

```
protein = 80 × 2.2  = 176 g → 704 kcal
fat     = 100 × 0.7 = 70 g  → 630 kcal
carbs   = (2500 − 704 − 630) ÷ 4 ≈ 292 g
```

That is the split the [macro calculator](/tools/macro-calculator) returns for
the same inputs.

## Consistency beats precision

You don't need to hit grams exactly. Land within ~5–10 g of protein and keep
calories in range most days, and you'll progress. Track for a few weeks to build
intuition, not forever; [tracking food](/docs/tracking-food) covers raw versus
cooked weights and reading labels.
