Weight, tape and mirror rarely agree in the same week, and that is fine: each
answers a different question. Read them together, over the right stretch of
time, and you know whether to keep going or to adjust, without letting one
number on the scale decide your mood.

## Read the weekly average, not the day

A single morning tells you about yesterday's salt, sleep and dinner. Weigh in
on waking, fasted, after the bathroom, and compare this week's average with
last week's, never one Monday with the next. Three weigh-ins a week is the
minimum for an average worth reading; seven is better. The Metri app works the
same way: a week with fewer than three weigh-ins is shown but not judged.
[Weight tracking](/docs/weight-tracking) has the full method.

## Water moves faster than fat

A kilo of body-fat tissue holds roughly **7,000–7,700 kcal**, which caps how
much fat one day can add: 1,000 kcal over maintenance is about 0.13 kg of fat
at most. A jump of 1–2 kg overnight is water, glycogen and food still in the
gut, and it leaves the way it came.

| Cause                       | What is happening                                              |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Big, salty or late meal     | Sodium holds water and the food still weighs; gone in 2–3 days |
| More carbs than usual       | Glycogen refills, storing ~3 g of water per gram               |
| Cutting carbs hard          | The same glycogen and water leaving: a fast 1–2 kg, not fat    |
| Menstrual cycle             | Fluid rises in the days around a period                        |
| New program or a hard block | Sore muscles hold water while they repair                      |
| Starting creatine           | Muscle draws in water over the first weeks; ~1 kg is common    |
| Travel, poor sleep, stress  | Fluid retention and irregular digestion                        |

## How long before a trend counts

| Signal                | Judge after | Noise to ignore            |
| --------------------- | ----------- | -------------------------- |
| Weekly average weight | 2–3 weeks   | ±0.5 kg week to week       |
| Tape (waist, hips)    | 3–4 weeks   | ±0.5–1 cm between readings |
| Photos                | 4–8 weeks   | Light, pump, posture       |
| Lifts                 | 3–4 weeks   | One bad session            |

If you menstruate, compare the same week of each cycle, or judge across a whole
cycle. A change that shows on one signal for one week is not a trend yet.

## The four situations

| Scale     | Tape | What is happening                  |
| --------- | ---- | ---------------------------------- |
| Up        | Down | Recomposition: muscle up, fat down |
| Down      | Flat | Water early on; muscle if it lasts |
| Flat      | Up   | Fat gain at the same weight        |
| Down fast | Flat | Water and glycogen, or muscle loss |

"Tape" means the sites where you store fat first: the waist for most men; hips,
thighs and glutes for most women. Those are the sites the Metri app pairs with
your weight trend (waist for men, hips for women); keep the others as context.

## 1. Weight up, measurements down

Common in the first one or two months for someone new to lifting, or who just
started training properly. Waist and hips shrink while the scale climbs: some
fat left, and muscles that suddenly have work to do filled up with glycogen and
water, plus a little new tissue. Most of that early gain is fuel and water, not
kilos of new muscle, but the direction is exactly right.

**What to do:** nothing. Keep the plan. Expect it to fade after the second
month, when total weight should start moving down in a cut.

## 2. Weight down, measurements flat

The scale drops, but waist, arms and legs stay put and the mirror looks the
same. For a week or two that is usually water, and the tape catches up. If it
goes on for a month while your lifts fall, the loss is coming from muscle, not
fat.

**What to do:** check the basics: protein at [target](/docs/macros), sleep,
effort in the gym. Slow the deficit (see [weight tracking](/docs/weight-tracking))
and give it two weeks.

## 3. Weight flat, measurements up

Typical in someone trying to gain who overeats: the waist grows while the scale
barely moves. If the weight is truly flat there is no real surplus on average,
so fat is taking the place of lean tissue, the reverse of a recomposition; if
it creeps up a little, the surplus is going to fat. Either way, the extra food
is not turning into muscle, and chronic overeating, especially of processed
food, also worsens [insulin sensitivity](/docs/insulin-sensitivity).

**What to do:** rule out the obvious first (measure fasted, same spot, same
time, and allow for the noise above). Then do not add food to push the scale.
Review [diet quality](/docs/diet-quality), check protein and training effort,
keep steps up, and check the bulking ceiling in
[Cut, bulk or maintain?](/docs/choosing-your-phase). Near the ceiling, move to
maintenance or a light deficit.

## 4. Big weight drop, no visual change

Two very different causes:

- **You just cut carbs.** A low-carb or ketogenic start empties muscle glycogen
  and the water bound to it: 1–2 kg vanish in a week with nothing to show in
  the mirror. Expected, not a problem.
- **Normal diet, drops for weeks or months, same look.** Muscle may be going,
  especially if your lifts are falling too. Muscle lost in a diet is slow to
  rebuild, and the expenditure it carried goes with it, which is how the weight
  comes back.

**What to do:** in the first case, wait. In the second, treat it as situation 2.

## The case that worries people most

"I weigh the same or slightly more and it bothers me, but my clothes fit better
and the tape is down." That is muscle gained and fat lost. Of the three signals
the scale is the least informative; trust the tape and the photos.

The mirror is the least reliable judge day to day, because you see yourself
every day. Photos taken four or more weeks apart, in the same light and pose,
are not: put them side by side. Clothes that fit differently at the waist are
the same evidence for free. The waist also matters for health on its own: under
half your height is the usual target, and the
[waist-to-height calculator](/tools/waist-to-height-ratio-calculator) does the
arithmetic.

  A single week never decides. Compare 4-week averages of weight and tape
  before changing anything.
