Few supplements have real evidence behind them, and the effect of the ones that
do is small. Get food, training and sleep right first; then, if you want, pick
from this short list. Don't obsess over it.

## The short list

| Supplement                  | Dose       | When                                    |
| --------------------------- | ---------- | --------------------------------------- |
| **Creatine monohydrate**    | 5 g/day    | Every day, training or not, any time    |
| **Whey protein**            | 20–30 g    | Only to reach your daily protein target |
| **Caffeine**                | 100–200 mg | Before training, 3–4×/week at most      |
| **Multivitamin** (optional) | Per label  | With a meal                             |
| **Melatonin** (optional)    | 0.5–5 mg   | Shortly before bed                      |

## Creatine

The most studied supplement and the cheapest per gram. It improves performance
and recovery and draws water into the muscle. Take **5 g every day**, rest days
included, at any time, dissolved in any liquid: no loading phase, no cycling,
no timing tricks. Skip it and you lose nothing but a few percent of
performance.

## Whey protein

Whey is food in powder form: a scoop is roughly **80% protein by weight**, so
30 g of powder gives about 24 g of protein. Count it like any other protein
source and use it only for the gap between what you eat and your target; see
the [macros guide](/docs/macros). Any reputable whey or isolate works.

## Caffeine

It raises performance in the gym (and at the desk), but daily use blunts part
of the effect, so keep it to **3–4 days a week**. Have the last dose about
eight hours before bed (around 15:00 for a 23:00 bedtime): it is still in your
system long after you stop feeling it. Count everything, pre-workouts and
energy drinks included.

## Optional extras

- **Multivitamin:** insurance against micronutrient gaps, not a substitute for
  vegetables and fruit. If your diet is varied you may not notice a difference.
- **Melatonin:** a timing signal, not a sleeping pill. It helps most with jet
  lag and shifted schedules and shortens the time to fall asleep by minutes,
  not hours; low doses work as well as high ones. If you need it every night,
  fix the basics first; see
  [Improve your sleep quality](/docs/sleep-quality). In some countries it is
  prescription-only.

<Callout type="info">
  Food first. Supplements fill gaps; they don't replace calories, protein or
  sleep.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  Taking medication, pregnant, or managing a health condition? Ask a doctor or
  pharmacist before adding anything. Prefer products with third-party testing,
  since supplements are less tightly regulated than medicines.
</Callout>
