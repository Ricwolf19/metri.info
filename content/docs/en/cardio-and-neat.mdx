Cardio and daily movement serve heart health and energy balance; they are not
where muscle comes from. The useful questions are how much you need for your
goal, and which kind costs you the least recovery.

## NEAT: steps first

**NEAT** (non-exercise activity thermogenesis) is everything you burn outside
training, mostly walking. It is the cheapest tool you have: it raises daily
expenditure, supports insulin sensitivity, and accumulates almost no fatigue,
so it never competes with your lifting.

| Goal                 | Daily steps   |
| -------------------- | ------------- |
| Bulking              | 7,000–8,000   |
| Cutting / recomp     | 8,000–10,000  |
| Any goal, for health | 7,500 or more |

Around 7,500 steps a day is where observational studies show a markedly lower
all-cause mortality. More still helps, but the curve flattens.

## Cardio while bulking

Keep it recreational: **30–40 min of moderate cardio, 1–2 times a week**, if
you enjoy it. It supports cardiovascular health and insulin sensitivity, so a
surplus turns into less fat. Beyond that it starts to eat into recovery from
lifting, and if the scale stops moving, adjust food, not cardio.

## Cardio while cutting

Hold a fixed dose and let the diet do the work: **2 sessions × 30–40 min
moderate** per week, the same every week. If you eat the same calories weekly,
move the same too; otherwise you cannot tell what changed when progress
stalls.

When fat loss genuinely plateaus (3–4 weeks, not one bad weigh-in), progress
cardio and steps the way you progress loads: small, gradual, over months.

| Stage                  | Cardio                | Steps        |
| ---------------------- | --------------------- | ------------ |
| Start                  | 2 × 30–40 min         | 7,000–8,000  |
| Each adjustment        | +10 min or +1 session | +1,000–2,000 |
| Months into a long cut | Up to 4 × 45 min      | Up to 15,000 |

Most cuts never need the last row. Once you are at 10,000–12,000 steps and
1.5–2 h of easy cardio a week, trim food a little or train harder before adding
more; see [breaking a plateau](/docs/breaking-a-plateau).

<Callout type="tip">
  Add steps before adding cardio. Walking costs no recovery; cardio does.
</Callout>

## The fasted cardio myth

Fasted, you burn a higher share of fat *during* the session; afterwards the
body leans more on glycogen and less on fat, and fed, the reverse happens. Some
24-hour studies still find slightly more fat oxidised on fasted days, but
trials lasting weeks find no difference in body fat lost.

- 40 min at a moderate pace ≈ **400 kcal**, fasted or fed.
- What moves fat loss is the **daily energy deficit**.
- Do it fasted only if you like it. Fed, if that buys you a better session.

[Fat-loss myths](/docs/fat-loss-myths) walks through the evidence.

## Heart-rate zones

"Moderate" means you can still talk in short sentences: roughly 60–75% of your
maximum heart rate. The
[heart-rate zone calculator](/tools/heart-rate-zone-calculator) gives your
zones, and the

a session costs.
